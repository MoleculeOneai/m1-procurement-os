# Mode: Supplier ESG Assessment

## Goal
Rate suppliers on ESG using evidence, focus effort on the highest-risk ones, and agree improvement actions.

## Step-by-Step

**Step 1 — Prioritise.** For a supplier base, screen first by inherent risk: category (for example apparel, electronics, mining, agriculture, contract labour and construction carry higher labour and environmental exposure), sourcing country, spend, and criticality. Assess in depth only the High-inherent-risk suppliers and strategic ones. State the screening logic.

**Step 2 — Select themes and questions.** Use the user's priority themes. Standard areas:

| Area | Example evidence |
|---|---|
| Environment | Emissions data and targets, energy and water use, waste, environmental management certification, biodiversity and deforestation policy where relevant |
| Labour and human rights | Policy on forced and child labour, working-hours and wage practice, freedom of association, recruitment-fee policy, grievance mechanism, audit reports |
| Health and safety | Incident rates, safety management certification, training |
| Business ethics | Anti-bribery policy, whistle-blowing, sanctions and trade compliance, conflict-minerals or sourcing-origin controls where relevant |
| Governance and transparency | Public reporting, board oversight, supply-chain mapping, subcontractor controls |
| Diversity and local impact | Supplier diversity, community programmes (only if the user cares about it) |

Tailor the questionnaire to the category. Do not send the same 80 questions to every supplier.

**Step 3 — Score on evidence.** For each area use a four-point scale:
- **3 Advanced** — documented policy, measurable targets, independent verification
- **2 Established** — documented policy and practice, no independent verification
- **1 Basic** — informal or partial
- **0 Not evidenced** — no evidence supplied (never score this as 1 or 2)

Weight areas by the user's priorities (default equal weights, shown to the user). Calculate an overall score and a band: Green (strong), Amber (gaps), Red (serious concern). Any single Red-flag finding, such as evidence of forced labour or a sanctions breach, overrides the overall score and triggers escalation.

**Step 4 — Treat self-declaration with care.** Mark which answers are self-declared and which are verified by a certificate, third-party audit or rating. Certificates have scope and expiry. Check what they actually cover.

**Step 5 — Build the improvement plan.** For each gap: the action, the evidence required, owner, due date, and the consequence of non-delivery (watch status, hold on new business, exit). Corrective-action plans first, termination as the last step, unless the finding is severe.

**Step 6 — Output.** Supplier ESG scorecard, evidence table, red flags, improvement plan and re-assessment date. For a portfolio view: heat-map table by supplier and area, plus counts by band.

## Output Rules
- Show the data behind each score. A rating without evidence is an opinion.
- Do not quote a third-party rating for a named company from memory. Use reports the user supplies.
- Keep the supplier-facing summary free of internal weights and escalation rules.
