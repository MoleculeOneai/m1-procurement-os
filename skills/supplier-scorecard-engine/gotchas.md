# Gotchas — Supplier Scorecard Engine

Add a line each time Claude trips on something new.

---

## Scorecard Design Gotchas

- **Criteria with no data source are scoring theatre.** If you include a criterion that no one can actually measure — because the data doesn't exist, isn't collected, or requires a survey that never gets sent — it will either be left blank or filled with guesswork. Before finalising any scorecard, ask: "Where will the data for this criterion come from, and how often?" If the answer is "we'll chase the supplier for it", remove the criterion or replace it with something observable.
- **Weighting every dimension equally is a cop-out.** Equal weights signal that no one made a real decision about what matters. Always force a prioritisation conversation. For most categories, quality/delivery is worth more than sustainability scores. Push back if the user proposes 10 criteria all weighted 10%.
- **Don't score what you don't manage.** Scorecards with 20+ criteria sound comprehensive but become impossible to run consistently. The optimum is 6–10 scoreable dimensions. More than 10 usually means the team hasn't agreed on priorities.
- **Category-specific criteria matter.** A logistics scorecard that uses the same criteria as a professional services scorecard is wrong. On-time delivery is critical for logistics; it's irrelevant for consulting. Always tailor the criteria to the category.
- **Innovation and ESG criteria need a definition agreed upfront.** "Innovation" scored without a definition gets whatever the reviewer feels like giving. Define what a score of 1, 3, and 5 looks like before anyone uses the scorecard.

## Scoring Session Gotchas

- **Never populate scores without a named reviewer.** Anonymous scores cannot be challenged or audited. Always capture who provided the scores and on what date. This is especially important if scores are later disputed by the supplier.
- **Scores entered as verbal descriptions need anchoring.** If someone says "they're pretty good on delivery", that is not a 4 or a 5 until you know what "pretty good" means in this context. Always anchor: "On a scale of 1–5 where 5 is 100% on-time in full for the period, what score would you give?"
- **Binary SLA compliance is not the same as a performance score.** "Did they meet SLA?" (yes/no) and "How well did they perform?" (1–5) are different questions. A supplier who hits 80% of SLAs is not the same as one who hit 99%. Use actual metrics where available — not just pass/fail.
- **Missing data must be flagged, not zero-scored.** If a criterion has no data available for the period, record it as N/A and exclude it from the weighted total. Never score missing data as zero — it will unfairly drag the overall rating down.
- **Recency bias corrupts trend scores.** If a supplier had one very good delivery last week after six mediocre months, the reviewer may want to score them highly. Always score the full review period, not just recent memory.
- **Self-scored supplier inputs need calibration.** If the supplier provides their own performance data, never use it without reconciling it against buyer-side records. Discrepancies are common and revealing.

## Reporting & Action Gotchas

- **A scorecard without a consequence is a vanity exercise.** If Green suppliers get no recognition and Red suppliers face no action, the scorecard achieves nothing. Always confirm: "What happens when a supplier scores Red? What happens when they score Green?"
- **Never share a draft scorecard with a supplier.** Internal working drafts contain reviewer comments and context that can damage the relationship if seen. Only share finalised, approved reports — and only the summary view, not the full internal working file.
- **Red ratings require a conversation, not just a document.** Sending a Red scorecard by email without a follow-up call is a relationship management failure. Always recommend a supplier review meeting within 10 business days of issuing a Red rating.
- **Improvement plans need buy-in from both sides.** A supplier improvement plan written entirely by the buyer and handed to the supplier will not be owned or actioned. Good plans are co-created. Flag this if the user wants to write an improvement plan unilaterally.
- **Trend analysis needs at least 3 data points.** "They've improved" or "they're declining" with only two periods of data is not a trend — it's noise. Flag this when a user asks for trend analysis on fewer than three scoring periods.
- **Benchmarking against peers requires normalised scorecards.** You cannot compare two suppliers scored on different criteria or different scales. Always check that the scorecard version is the same before running a benchmark comparison.

## Contract & Governance Gotchas

- **SLAs in contracts often don't match scorecard criteria.** It's common for procurement to build a scorecard using criteria that aren't actually defined in the contract. Before scoring, check: "Are these criteria aligned with the contractual SLAs?" If not, flag the gap — you may be tracking the wrong things.
- **Score results may be discoverable in disputes.** In the event of a contract dispute, scorecard records can be requested as evidence. Never include informal commentary or opinions in the formal scorecard record. Keep it factual and measurable.
