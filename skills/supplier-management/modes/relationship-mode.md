# Mode: Relationship Management, Tiering and Exit

## Goal
Decide how much management effort each supplier deserves, run structured reviews, and plan development or exit when needed.

## Step-by-Step

**Step 1 — Tier the suppliers.** Use the organisation's tiers from config. If none, propose:

| Tier | Typical profile | Management approach |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | High spend or business criticality, hard to replace (Kraljic strategic or leverage with high criticality) | Executive sponsor, joint planning, quarterly business reviews, innovation and risk sharing |
| Preferred | Meaningful spend, some alternatives | Annual or semi-annual review, scorecard, relationship owner |
| Transactional | Low value, easily replaced | Contract compliance, annual check, consolidate where possible |
| Watch | Any tier with open Red or Critical findings | Remediation plan with dates and a decision point |

Use `category-strategy-builder` quadrants and `supplier-scorecard-engine` results where they exist. State the criteria used so the tiering can be repeated.

**Step 2 — Assign relationship ownership.** For each Strategic and Preferred supplier: business owner, procurement owner, supplier counterpart, review cadence.

**Step 3 — Prepare a business review (when asked).** Agenda: performance against scorecard and SLAs, commercial position (spend, price vs market, savings delivered), risk and compliance status, ESG commitments, innovation and improvement ideas, issues and actions from last review, plan for next period. Draft talking points for both the supportive and the difficult messages.

**Step 4 — Plan development or remediation.** For underperforming suppliers: the gap, root cause (supplier, our process, or specification), the improvement actions with measures and dates, the support we will give, and the trigger for escalation.

**Step 5 — Plan an exit when needed.** Cover the reason, contractual route and notice (check with `contract-lifecycle-manager`), replacement options and readiness, transition plan (data, knowledge, inventory, in-flight orders), risks during transition, and communication plan. A plan with no ready alternative is a risk, not an exit.

**Step 6 — Output.** Tiered supplier list, ownership table, review calendar, and the relevant review pack or plan.

## Output Rules
- Do not over-manage the tail. If more than about 10% of suppliers are Strategic, challenge the tiering.
- Keep supplier-facing material free of internal leverage notes, targets and fallback positions.
